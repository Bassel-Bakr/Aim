Topics are signed pages: one person's take on a subject, written from experience rather than
assembled from sources. Every page here carries its author's name at the top, and that name is the
point.

They sit outside the [wiki](../wiki/index.md) on purpose, because they run on a different trust
model. A wiki page earns trust by citing a public source for each claim, and anyone can correct it.
A topic page earns trust by carrying its author's name. That is the whole model: if you trust the
person who wrote it, the page is worth your time, and if you do not, it is not. Judge it on the
name, and on whether the method works when you try it.

Because they are opinions, expect them to disagree with each other eventually, and to disagree with
how you already train. Nothing here overrides the wiki's sourced pages on
[Fundamentals](../wiki/fundamentals/index.md) or [Skills](../wiki/skills/index.md); a topic page is
one person's route through that material, not a correction to it.

## Writing one

Topics are written by this site's maintainer. Authorship is not open to contributors, so open an
issue on the [source repository](https://github.com/Bassel-Bakr/aim) rather than a pull request
that adds one. Corrections to a published page, such as a factual error, a dead link, or a typo,
are welcome from anyone; changes to the method or the argument go to the author, because the byline
has to keep meaning what it says.

The wiki is the opposite: it is open to contributions, and
[CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/Bassel-Bakr/aim/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) covers how to add or
change a page there.
