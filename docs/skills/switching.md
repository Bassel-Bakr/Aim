Switching is the skill of moving your crosshair from a target you've just cleared to the next one,
closing that gap quickly without losing the accuracy you need to land the next click.
[Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md) calls it "the heart of dynamic decision-making in
aiming," where focus transfers from target to target in a swift, seamless motion, per
[Introduction to Aim Training – The Basics of Aimlabs](https://aimlabs.com/articles/aimlabs/introduction-to-aim-training-the-basics-of-aimlabs/).
Aimlabs' own generic split for this category is simpler, static and dynamic switching, per the
same article; the three subcategories below, speed, evasive, and stability, follow the finer split
[Voltaic](../resources/communities/voltaic.md) uses in its Season 5 KovaaK's benchmark, per the
[Season 5 announcement](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/).

## Speed switching

Speed switching tests how fast you can move between a wide field of small-to-medium targets.
Voltaic's DotTS scenario spreads its dots across a wide field of view specifically to force big,
fast transitions between them, per the
[Season 5 announcement](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/).
Good execution is a flick straight to the next target followed by a quick micro-adjustment to
settle on it, rather than a slow drift across the gap. The common mistake is chasing raw speed
before the flick itself is accurate, which raises your miss rate faster than it raises your kill
rate; the [speed-accuracy trade-off](../fundamentals/how-aim-works.md) applies directly here.
Train it by widening target spacing gradually rather than starting at a spacing where you can't
land most switches, and treat accuracy, not time, as the number to improve first.

## Evasive switching

Evasive switching adds targets that keep moving after you've switched onto them, so a good switch
still needs a beat of tracking to close it out. Voltaic's DriftTS scenario places its spheres at a
mix of ranges and has them accelerate away as they drift, which is what forces that follow-up
tracking, per the
[Season 5 announcement](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/).
Aimlabs' guidance for tracking and switching onto moving targets is to start tracking a target as
it changes direction, since that gives the best chance to stay smooth and finish it off, and to
avoid pulling off a target too early once you've committed to it, per
[Tracking and Switching Moving Targets in Apex Legends](https://aimlabs.com/articles/aimlabs/tracking-and-switching-moving-targets-in-apex-legends/).
The common mistake is exactly that: bailing on a target the instant it gets harder to hit and
jumping to an easier one, which trades one clean kill for a scattered miss. Train it on scenarios
with clearly telegraphed target motion before moving on to more erratic evasive movement.

## Stability switching

Stability switching is Voltaic's Season 5 hybrid category bridging switching and tracking. Its
ControlTS scenario looks a lot like speed switching, small spheres to flick between, but each one
hops slightly as it sits there, per the
[Season 5 announcement](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/),
which describes the season's hybrid categories as "bridging traditional subcategory gaps." Good
execution is a stable flick onto each target that holds through its small hop, instead of a flick
that immediately drifts off as the target moves. The common mistake is treating the switch as
finished the moment the crosshair arrives, then losing the target as soon as it moves. Since this
category blends switching with tracking, alternating drills between speed switching at a wider
spacing and [Tracking](tracking.md) at a slower pace builds both halves before combining them here.
