Tracking is the skill of keeping your crosshair on a target that's already moving, rather than
acquiring it fresh or hopping to the next one. [Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md)
describes it as making sure your "crosshair remains glued to your opponents," which matters most
in longer engagements where a target stays alive and mobile, per
[Introduction to Aim Training – The Basics of Aimlabs](https://aimlabs.com/articles/aimlabs/introduction-to-aim-training-the-basics-of-aimlabs/).
The three subcategories below, precise, reactive, and control, follow
[Voltaic](../resources/communities/voltaic.md)'s Season 5 KovaaK's benchmark, per the
[Season 5 announcement](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/).

## Precise tracking

Precise tracking follows a target whose movement is smooth and largely predictable, so the
challenge is staying exactly on it rather than reading sudden direction changes. Voltaic's PGT
scenario is one example: a small, curved target the player has to approach and stay glued to
rather than one that darts unpredictably, per the
[Season 5 announcement](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/).
Aimlabs' own routines describe the equivalent skill as "pure horizontal tracking and smoothness,"
emphasizing precision while mimicking realistic in-game movement, per
[Master Tracking and Switching with the Apex Legends Aim Basics Routine](https://aimlabs.com/articles/aim-basics-routines/master-tracking-and-switching-with-the-apex-legends-aim-basics-routine/).
Good execution means the crosshair moves at the same rate as the target instead of lagging and
snapping forward to catch up, the smoothness quality covered in more depth on
[How Aim Works](../fundamentals/how-aim-works.md). A common mistake is over-correcting a small
mismatch, which turns a smooth track into a series of jerky snaps. Train it on slower, predictable
paths first so you can hold a clean, continuous track before adding speed or unpredictability.

## Reactive tracking

Reactive tracking follows a target that changes direction and speed less predictably, so it's less
about smoothness and more about reading each change as it happens. Voltaic's Aether scenario builds
this by having the target dodge on two axes at once, up-down as well as left-right, at speeds that
keep shifting, per the
[Season 5 announcement](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/).
Aimlabs frames the equivalent skill as "raw reactivity and target reading" rather than smoothness,
per
[Master Tracking and Switching with the Apex Legends Aim Basics Routine](https://aimlabs.com/articles/aim-basics-routines/master-tracking-and-switching-with-the-apex-legends-aim-basics-routine/).
Good execution means tracking where the target actually is rather than anticipating where it's
going, since prediction looks tighter right up until an opponent changes direction to bait it, at
which point it fails outright; see [How Aim Works](../fundamentals/how-aim-works.md) on reacting
versus predicting. The common mistake here is exactly that: predicting a pattern instead of
reacting to it, which works only until the target breaks the pattern. Train it with scenarios that
deliberately vary target direction, and treat any target whose path you can fully predict as too
easy to be useful practice for this skill specifically.

## Control tracking

Control tracking is Voltaic's Season 5 hybrid category bridging precise and reactive tracking. Its
Raw Control scenario has the target move across all three spatial axes at speeds that keep
changing, but its turns are rounded off rather than sharp, splitting the difference between
precise tracking's predictability and reactive tracking's abruptness, per the
[Season 5 announcement](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/),
which describes the season's hybrid categories as "bridging traditional subcategory gaps." Good
execution blends the two skills above: staying smooth through the readable parts of the target's
path while still reacting cleanly when its direction changes. The common mistake is defaulting
entirely to one mode, either smoothing through direction changes you should be reacting to, or
over-reacting to motion that's actually smooth and predictable. [KovaaK's](../resources/trainers/kovaaks.md)
sells a dedicated Tracking Trainer DLC with eleven tracking-focused modules and dynamic difficulty,
built by the game's own developer, per
[its Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1438210/KovaaKs_Tracking_Trainer/), which is a
reasonable place to practice all three tracking subcategories.
