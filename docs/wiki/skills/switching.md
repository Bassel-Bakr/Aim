Switching is the skill of moving your crosshair from a target you've just cleared to the next one,
closing that gap quickly without losing the accuracy you need to land the next click.
[Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md) calls it "the heart of dynamic decision-making in
aiming," where focus transfers from target to target in a swift, seamless motion.[^aimlabs-intro]

Two things make one switch harder than another: how far apart the targets sit, and whether they
stay put once you arrive. Distance sets how fast the transition has to be; target motion decides
how much work is left after the flick lands. The sections below take those in turn, then combine
them.

## Stationary targets

The targets hold still, so the whole difficulty is the transition itself: how fast you can cross
the gap and still land the click. Good execution is a flick straight to the next target followed by
a quick micro-adjustment to settle on it, rather than a slow drift across the gap.

The common mistake is chasing raw speed before the flick itself is accurate, which raises your miss
rate faster than it raises your kill rate; the
[speed-accuracy trade-off](../fundamentals/how-aim-works.md) applies directly here. Train it by
widening target spacing gradually rather than starting at a spacing where you can't land most
switches, and treat accuracy, not time, as the number to improve first.

## Moving targets

The targets keep moving after you switch onto them, so a good switch still needs a beat of tracking
to close it out. Aimlabs' guidance is to start tracking a target as it changes direction, since
that gives the best chance to stay smooth and finish it off, and to avoid pulling off a target too
early once you've committed to it.[^aimlabs-apex-moving]

The common mistake is exactly that: bailing on a target the instant it gets harder to hit and
jumping to an easier one, which trades one clean kill for a scattered miss. Train it on scenarios
with clearly telegraphed target motion before moving on to more erratic movement.

## Unstable targets

Between the two sits the case where targets barely move at all, hopping or jittering slightly in
place. It looks like switching between stationary targets, but the crosshair has to hold through
that small movement rather than arriving and stopping. Good execution is a stable flick onto each
target that holds through the hop, instead of one that immediately drifts off.

The common mistake is treating the switch as finished the moment the crosshair arrives, then losing
the target as soon as it moves. Because this blends switching with tracking, alternating drills
between wide-spaced stationary switching and [Tracking](tracking.md) at a slower pace builds both
halves before combining them.

## How communities name these

[Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md) uses a two-way split, static and dynamic
switching.[^aimlabs-intro] [Voltaic](../resources/communities/voltaic.md)'s Season 5 KovaaK's
benchmark uses speed, evasive, and stability, the last added as a hybrid category "bridging
traditional subcategory gaps."[^voltaic-s5] The labels change between benchmark seasons; target
spacing and target motion are what the categories are actually built on.
