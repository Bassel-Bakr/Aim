Short definitions of the aim-training terms used across this wiki, in our own words, each linked
to the page that explains it in more depth.

### Benchmark

A benchmark is a fixed set of scenarios you run once, compare against a wider player base, and
re-run later to check whether you are actually improving, rather than judging a session by feel.
See [Benchmarks](training/benchmarks.md).

### Clicking

Clicking is the skill of acquiring a target and landing a single shot or short burst on it. Dynamic
clicking targets something already moving, static clicking a stationary target, and linear clicking
a hybrid where the target moves along a single predictable line. See [Clicking](skills/clicking.md).

### cm/360

cm/360 is the physical distance your mouse has to travel across the pad to turn your in-game view a
full 360 degrees. Because it is measured in real-world distance, it is comparable across any game,
engine, or mouse, unlike raw DPI or sensitivity numbers on their own. See
[Sensitivity](getting-started/sensitivity.md).

### DPI

DPI (dots per inch) is how far your mouse cursor moves on screen per inch of physical mouse
movement, set on the mouse itself rather than in a game. On its own it does not tell you how a
sensitivity feels in a given game; eDPI and cm/360 exist to make it comparable. See
[Sensitivity](getting-started/sensitivity.md).

### eDPI

eDPI (effective DPI) is your mouse DPI multiplied by your in-game sensitivity multiplier. Two
players with the same eDPI move their crosshair the same amount for the same hand movement in that
specific game, but eDPI is only comparable within one game, not across different games. See
[Sensitivity](getting-started/sensitivity.md).

### Flick

A flick is the fast, committed mouse motion that acquires a target you are not already on, the core
motion behind clicking scenarios. Good technique lands the flick on the target with only a small
correction left over, rather than a rough first pass fixed up afterward. See
[Clicking](skills/clicking.md).

### FOV

Field of view (FOV) is the extent of the game world visible on screen at once, usually set in
degrees.[^fov]

### FPS

FPS (frames per second) is how many frames your game renders each second. A frame rate that
comfortably exceeds your monitor's refresh rate measurably reduces input lag even with V-Sync off.
See [Setup and Gear](getting-started/setup.md).

### Hz

Hz (hertz) is a monitor's refresh rate, how many times per second it redraws the screen. A higher
refresh rate shortens the time between frames the display can show, for example a 240Hz display
refreshes noticeably faster per frame than a 144Hz one. See
[Setup and Gear](getting-started/setup.md).

### Micro-adjustment

A micro-adjustment is a small correction made mostly with the fingers and wrist rather than a large
arm-driven motion, used to settle precisely onto a target or hold a track. See
[How Aim Works](fundamentals/how-aim-works.md).

### Overflick

Overflicking (also called overshooting) is landing a flick past the target, so the crosshair has to
reverse direction and drag back onto it, which costs more correction time than landing slightly
short in the same direction.[^flick] See
[Clicking](skills/clicking.md) for how this wiki describes a clean flick.

### Playlist

A playlist is a pre-built list of scenarios, usually published by a trainer or community, that you
can run as-is or adapt instead of assembling a routine from nothing. See
[Routines](training/routines.md).

### Raw input

Raw input is a setting that lets a game read mouse movement more directly from the device instead
of relying only on the Windows pointer path, which makes sensitivity more predictable. See
[Setup and Gear](getting-started/setup.md).

### Routine

A routine is the specific set of scenarios you run in a session, in some order, for some length of
time, usually built around whichever skill a benchmark shows as your current weakness. See
[Routines](training/routines.md).

### Scenario

A scenario is a single drill inside an aim trainer built to isolate one specific mechanical skill,
such as tracking a moving target, rather than reproducing a full match. See
[Aim Trainers](getting-started/aim-trainers.md).

### Sensitivity

Sensitivity is how far your view turns for a given amount of physical mouse movement, set through a
combination of mouse DPI and an in-game multiplier. See
[Sensitivity](getting-started/sensitivity.md).

### Smoothness

Smoothness is whether your crosshair moves as one continuous motion that matches a target's speed,
rather than lagging behind and then snapping forward to catch up. It runs through tracking,
flicking, and switching alike rather than being a separate skill of its own. See
[How Aim Works](fundamentals/how-aim-works.md).

### Switching

Switching is the skill of moving your crosshair from a target you just cleared to the next
one, closing that gap quickly without losing the accuracy needed to land the next click. Speed
switching puts the weight on the transition itself, evasive switching adds targets that keep moving
after you arrive, and stability switching is a hybrid where targets hop or jitter in place. See
[Switching](skills/switching.md).

### Tracking

Tracking is the skill of keeping your crosshair on a target that is already moving, rather than
acquiring it fresh. Precise tracking follows smooth, largely predictable movement, reactive tracking
follows a target that changes direction and speed less predictably, and control tracking is a
hybrid of the two. See [Tracking](skills/tracking.md).

### Underflick

Underflicking (also called undershooting) is landing a flick just short of the target. It is
cheaper to fix than an overflick because your hand is already moving in the right direction and
only needs a small forward nudge, rather than a full reversal.[^flick] See
[Clicking](skills/clicking.md) for how this wiki describes a clean flick.

[^fov]: Wikipedia, [Field of view in video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_of_view_in_video_games)
[^flick]: aimbetween.games, [flick training](https://aimbetween.games/modes/flick)
