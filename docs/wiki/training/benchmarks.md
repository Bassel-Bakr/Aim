A benchmark is a standardized set of scenarios you run once, compare against a wider player base,
and re-run later to see whether you're actually getting better, rather than trusting how a session
felt.

## Explanation

**What a benchmark measures.** A benchmark is a fixed list of scenarios, split across
[Clicking](../skills/clicking.md), [Tracking](../skills/tracking.md), and
[Switching](../skills/switching.md) subcategories, that every player runs the same way so scores
are comparable across people and across your own attempts over time. The fixed list is the point:
change the scenarios and you lose the comparison, which is why benchmarks are versioned into
seasons rather than edited in place.

**Finding your weaknesses from it.** The value of running a benchmark isn't the single combined
rank at the bottom, it's the subcategory breakdown above it. Whichever subcategory scores lowest
relative to the others is clearer, less subjective evidence of where your aim needs work than a
hunch about what feels weak in a real game. Benchmarks that withhold a rank until you have a score
in every subcategory do so deliberately: it forces you to test the areas you would otherwise skip,
which are usually the ones worth training.

**How ranks usually work.** Most benchmarks group ranks into difficulty bands, so players at very
different levels each have a track to climb rather than sharing one ladder. Your placement is
typically driven by a combined score across subcategories, and how that score is combined matters
more than it looks: a scheme that weights your weakest subcategory more heavily than your strongest
rewards fixing weaknesses rather than grinding a strength. Rank names, thresholds, and verification
rules differ per benchmark and change between seasons, so read them from the benchmark's own site
rather than from any table reproduced elsewhere.

## The main benchmarks

Two communities run the benchmarks most players on this wiki will encounter, both built primarily
on [KovaaK's](../resources/trainers/kovaaks.md):

- [Voltaic](../resources/communities/voltaic.md) runs a Season 5 KovaaK's benchmark and a Season 3
  [Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md) one, each split into subcategories per skill across
  three difficulty bands: Novice, Intermediate, and Advanced.[^voltaic-s5][^voltaic-s3] A rank is
  granted only once you have a score in every subcategory, and is driven by an overall "Energy"
  score combining subcategories with a harmonic mean, which weights your weakest more heavily than
  your strongest.[^volt-lb]
- [Revosect](../resources/communities/revosect.md) runs a Season 5 KovaaK's benchmark covering six
  subcategories across the same three skills, with Entry, Intermediate, and Advanced tiers and a
  recorded VOD required to verify a score at the top tier.[^revo-bench]

Rank names and season numbering are independent between the two, so a rank on one does not
translate to a rank on the other. Smaller communities run their own benchmarks as well, and the
[Evxl Benchmark Tracker](../resources/tools/evxl.md) scores you against many of them at once.

## How often to rerun a benchmark

There's no fixed schedule that works for everyone, but running a full benchmark every session
usually wastes time you could spend training whatever weakness your last run found: a benchmark
measures where you are, it doesn't train you the way a focused [routine](routines.md) does. A gap
of a few weeks between full re-runs gives your training time to actually move the number, and it
keeps ordinary day-to-day variance, a bad night's sleep, an off session, from looking like a real
change in either direction.

## Why not to over-focus on rank

Treating the rank itself as the goal invites exactly the behavior the scoring is built to
discourage: finding one scenario you happen to be great at and grinding it for score instead of
training broadly. Where a benchmark weights your weakest subcategory most heavily, chasing a single
high score cannot substitute for fixing an actual weak area, see
[Practice Principles](../fundamentals/practice-principles.md). Treat your rank as a rough,
occasional check on whether your training is working, not as the thing you're training for.
