A benchmark is a standardized set of scenarios you run once, compare against a wider player base,
and re-run later. It shows whether you're actually getting better, rather than trusting how a
session felt.

- **Read the breakdown, not the rank.** Your lowest subcategory is where to train.
- **Re-run every few weeks.** Not every session.
- **Ranks don't translate.** A rank on one benchmark doesn't carry over to another.
- **The rank is a check, not the goal.** Grinding one strength won't fix a weakness.

## Explanation

**What a benchmark measures.** A benchmark is a fixed list of scenarios, split across
[Clicking](../categories/clicking.md), [Tracking](../categories/tracking.md), and
[Switching](../categories/switching.md) subcategories. Every player runs it the same way, so scores
are comparable across people and across your own attempts.

The fixed list is the point. Change the scenarios and you lose the comparison, which is why
benchmarks are versioned into seasons rather than edited in place.

**Finding your weaknesses from it.** The value isn't the single combined rank at the bottom. It's the
subcategory breakdown above it.

Whichever subcategory scores lowest relative to the others is clearer evidence of where your aim
needs work. It's less subjective than a hunch about what feels weak in a real game.

Some benchmarks withhold a rank until you have a score in every subcategory. That's deliberate: it
forces you to test the areas you'd otherwise skip, which are usually the ones worth training.

**How ranks usually work.** Most benchmarks group ranks into difficulty bands. Players at very
different levels each get a track to climb, rather than sharing one ladder.

Your placement is typically driven by a combined score across subcategories. How that score is
combined matters more than it looks.

A scheme that weights your weakest subcategory more heavily than your strongest rewards fixing
weaknesses, not grinding a strength.

Rank names, thresholds, and verification rules differ per benchmark and change between seasons. Read
them from the benchmark's own site, not from a table reproduced elsewhere.

## The main benchmarks

Two communities run the benchmarks most players on this wiki will encounter. Both build primarily on
[KovaaK's](../resources/trainers/kovaaks.md).

- [Voltaic](../resources/communities/voltaic.md) runs a Season 5 KovaaK's benchmark and a Season 3
  [Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md) one. Each splits into subcategories per category,
  across three difficulty bands: Novice, Intermediate, and Advanced.[^voltaic-s5][^voltaic-s3] A
  rank is granted only once you have a score in every subcategory. It's driven by an overall
  "Energy" score that combines subcategories with a harmonic mean, which weights your weakest more
  heavily than your strongest.[^volt-lb]
- [Revosect](../resources/communities/revosect.md) runs a Season 5 KovaaK's benchmark covering six
  subcategories across the same three categories. It has Entry, Intermediate, and Advanced tiers. A
  recorded VOD is required to verify a score at the top tier.[^revo-bench]

Rank names and season numbering are independent between the two, so a rank on one doesn't translate
to a rank on the other.

Smaller communities run their own benchmarks as well. The
[Evxl Benchmark Tracker](../resources/tools/evxl.md) scores you against many of them at once.

## How often to rerun a benchmark

There's no fixed schedule that works for everyone. But running a full benchmark every session
usually wastes time you could spend training the weakness your last run found.

A benchmark measures where you are. It doesn't train you the way a focused [routine](routines.md)
does.

A gap of a few weeks between full re-runs gives your training time to actually move the number. It
also keeps ordinary day-to-day variance, a bad night's sleep or an off session, from looking like a
real change in either direction.

## Why not to over-focus on rank

Treating the rank itself as the goal invites exactly the behavior the scoring is built to
discourage. That's finding one scenario you happen to be great at, and grinding it for score instead
of training broadly.

Where a benchmark weights your weakest subcategory most heavily, chasing a single high score can't
substitute for fixing an actual weak area. See
[Practice Principles](../fundamentals/practice-principles.md).

Treat your rank as a rough, occasional check on whether your training is working, not as the thing
you're training for.

**Do this next.** Run one full benchmark, note your lowest subcategory, and build your next
[routine](routines.md) around it.
