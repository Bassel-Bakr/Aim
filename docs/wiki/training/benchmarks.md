A benchmark is a standardized set of scenarios you run once, compare against a wider player base,
and re-run later to see whether you're actually getting better, rather than trusting how a session
felt. The best-known ones on this wiki come from [Voltaic](../resources/communities/voltaic.md)
and [Revosect](../resources/communities/revosect.md), both built primarily on
[KovaaK's](../resources/trainers/kovaaks.md), with Voltaic also running a version on
[Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md).

## Explanation

**What a benchmark measures.** A benchmark is a fixed list of scenarios, split across
[Clicking](../skills/clicking.md), [Tracking](../skills/tracking.md), and
[Switching](../skills/switching.md) subcategories, that every player runs the same way so scores
are comparable across people and across your own attempts over time. Voltaic's current KovaaK's
benchmark is Season 5 and its Aimlabs benchmark is Season 3, each split further into
precise/reactive/control-style subcategories per skill across three difficulty bands, per the
[Season 5 KovaaK's](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/)
and [Season 3 Aimlabs](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-3-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-aimlabs/)
announcements.
Revosect's current Season 5 KovaaK's benchmark covers six subcategories across the same three
skills, per its [benchmarks page](https://revosect.com/benchmarks).

**Finding your weaknesses from it.** The point of running a benchmark isn't the single combined
rank at the bottom, it's the subcategory breakdown above it: whichever subcategory scores lowest
relative to the others is clearer, less subjective evidence of where your aim needs work than a
hunch about what feels weak in a real game. Voltaic only grants a rank once you have a score in
every subcategory, per the
[Voltaic leaderboards page](https://app.voltaic.gg/leaderboards/about), so the benchmark itself
forces you to test areas you might otherwise skip.

**How Voltaic's ranks are structured.** Voltaic groups its ranks into three difficulty bands,
Novice, Intermediate, and Advanced, so players at very different skill levels have a track to
climb rather than one shared ladder, per the
[Season 5 announcement](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/).
Within that structure, your rank is driven by an overall "Energy" score that combines your
subcategory scores using a harmonic mean rather than a simple average, which weights your weakest
subcategory more heavily than your strongest, per the
[Voltaic leaderboards page](https://app.voltaic.gg/leaderboards/about). Exact rank names and score
thresholds live in Voltaic's app rather than its blog; see the
[official leaderboards](https://app.voltaic.gg/leaderboards) for current detail rather than
treating any rank table reproduced elsewhere as current.

**Revosect's ranks**, by contrast, use three tiers, Entry, Intermediate, and Advanced, and require
a recorded VOD to verify a score at the top Advanced tier, per
[Revosect](../resources/communities/revosect.md). Its rank names and season numbering are separate
from Voltaic's, so a rank on one benchmark doesn't translate directly to a rank on the other.

## How often to rerun a benchmark

There's no fixed schedule that works for everyone, but running a full benchmark every session
usually wastes time you could spend training whatever weakness your last run found: a benchmark
measures where you are, it doesn't train you the way a focused [routine](routines.md) does. A gap
of a few weeks between full re-runs gives your training time to actually move the number, and it
keeps ordinary day-to-day variance, a bad night's sleep, an off session, from looking like a real
change in either direction.

## Why not to over-focus on rank

Treating the rank itself as the goal invites exactly the behavior the scoring is built to
discourage: finding one scenario you happen to be great at and grinding it for score instead of
training broadly. Because Voltaic's Energy score weights your weakest subcategory more heavily
than your favorite one, chasing a single high score can't substitute for fixing an actual weak
area, see [Practice Principles](../fundamentals/practice-principles.md). Treat your rank as a
rough, occasional check on whether your training is working, not as the thing you're training for.
