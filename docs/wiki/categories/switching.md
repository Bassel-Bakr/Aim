Switching is the category covering moving your crosshair from a target you've just cleared to the next one,
closing that gap quickly without losing the accuracy you need to land the next click.
[Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md) calls it "the heart of dynamic decision-making in
aiming," where focus transfers from target to target in a swift, seamless motion.[^aimlabs-intro]

Two things make one switch harder than another: how far apart the targets sit, and how much they
move once you arrive. Distance sets how fast the transition has to be; target motion decides how
much work is left after the flick lands. Aimlabs splits switching two ways, static and
dynamic;[^aimlabs-intro] [Voltaic](../resources/communities/voltaic.md)'s Season 5 KovaaK's
benchmark names the three cases below speed, evasive, and stability.[^voltaic-s5]

## Speed switching

Speed switching puts the weight on the transition itself: how fast you can cross the gap and still
land the click. The targets are not necessarily frozen, but they move little enough that holding one
is never the hard part. Good execution is a flick straight to the next target followed by a quick
micro-adjustment to settle on it, rather than a slow drift across the gap.

The common mistake is chasing raw speed before the flick itself is accurate, which raises your miss
rate faster than it raises your kill rate; the
[speed-accuracy trade-off](../fundamentals/how-aim-works.md) applies directly here. Train it by
widening target spacing gradually rather than starting at a spacing where you can't land most
switches, and treat accuracy, not time, as the number to improve first.

## Evasive switching

Evasive switching is the moving case: the targets keep moving after you switch onto them, so a good
switch still needs a beat of tracking to close it out. Aimlabs' guidance is to start tracking a
target as it changes direction, since that gives the best chance to stay smooth and finish it off,
and to avoid pulling off a target too early once you've committed to it.[^aimlabs-apex-moving]

The common mistake is exactly that: bailing on a target the instant it gets harder to hit and
jumping to an easier one, which trades one clean kill for a scattered miss. Train it on scenarios
with clearly telegraphed target motion before moving on to more erratic movement.

## Stability switching

Stability switching sits between the two, added by Voltaic in Season 5 as a hybrid category
"bridging traditional subcategory gaps":[^voltaic-s5] the targets barely move at all, hopping or
jittering slightly in place. It looks like switching between stationary targets, but the crosshair
has to hold through that small movement rather than arriving and stopping. Good execution is a
stable flick onto each target that holds through the hop, instead of one that immediately drifts
off.

The common mistake is treating the switch as finished the moment the crosshair arrives, then losing
the target as soon as it moves. Because this blends switching with tracking, alternating drills
between wide-spaced stationary switching and [Tracking](tracking.md) at a slower pace builds both
halves before combining them.
