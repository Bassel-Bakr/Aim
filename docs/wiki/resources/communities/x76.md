## What it is

x76 is a player-run community built around arena and other competitive shooters. It hosts game
servers, keeps an Elo ladder from matches played on them, and runs a Discord community.[^REF-58]

It also publishes a wiki of guides, configs and a glossary, and sells coaching and gear.[^REF-58]

## Who it suits

Players of arena shooters such as Quake Live, especially lightning gun duels, who want servers to
play on and a ladder to measure themselves against.[^REF-58]

Its wiki suits anyone who aims while moving. The guides are written from in-game play rather than
from aim trainers.[^REF-59]

## What it covers

- **Servers and ladder**: servers across several games and regions, with an Elo ladder built from
  games played on them.[^REF-58]
- **Wiki guides**: guides on strafe aiming, angles, positioning and dodging, plus setup guides and
  configs for games including Quake Live.[^REF-59]
- **Glossary**: aim and movement terms for arena shooters. Some entries come from open sources such
  as [Voltaic](voltaic.md) and [Revosect](revosect.md), per the wiki's welcome page.[^REF-60]
- **Coaching**: paid live sessions for games including Deadlock, Overwatch and Apex Legends, and for
  aim training.[^REF-58]

## Key content

- [Strafe Aim Guide](https://x76.gg/wiki/strafe-aim-guide/): defines strafe aiming as movement that
  helps you aim, then works through its forms from basic to advanced.[^REF-61]
- [x76 wiki](https://x76.gg/wiki/): every guide, config and glossary entry, grouped by topic.

## Our take

x76 is a game community first. Its center is servers, a ladder and arena-shooter play, not
scenarios in an aim trainer.

Its wiki is the part most useful alongside this one. The strafe-aim guide covers aiming while both
you and your target move, which this wiki's pages do not. Its glossary overlaps this wiki's, and
credits some of the same community sources.
