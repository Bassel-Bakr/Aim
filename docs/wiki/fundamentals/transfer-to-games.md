An aim trainer is very good at one thing: giving you huge numbers of repetitions on the raw
mechanics of moving a crosshair. A real match asks for a lot more than that. This page separates
what trainer practice reliably improves from what it does not touch, so you know what still needs
practice in the game itself.

## Explanation

**What aim trainers train well: mechanics.** [KovaaK's](../resources/trainers/kovaaks.md) and
[Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md) both build their scenario libraries around isolating
one mechanical skill at a time, [Clicking](../skills/clicking.md), [Tracking](../skills/tracking.md),
or [Switching](../skills/switching.md), so you can put in far more focused reps on, say, tracking a
moving target than any single match would give you, per the
[KovaaK's](../resources/trainers/kovaaks.md) and [Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md)
resource pages. Benchmark communities such as [Voltaic](../resources/communities/voltaic.md) build
tiered rankings directly on top of this same split, which is a reasonable way to measure whether
your raw mouse control is improving over time.

**What they don't train.** A scenario doesn't reproduce the recoil pattern, movement, hitboxes, or
crosshair you actually use in a match, so what you practice in a trainer isolates mechanics rather
than transferring one-to-one, as covered in [Aim Trainers](../getting-started/aim-trainers.md).
Crosshair placement (keeping your aim where a target is about to appear) and positioning (where
you stand and how you move through a map) depend on that specific game's angles, sightlines, and
abilities, which no generic scenario reproduces. Even Voltaic, whose own benchmarks measure raw
aim, is explicit that its benchmarks "represent just one piece of the puzzle for in-game
performance" and should be paired with game sense and movement practiced in the actual game, per
its [Season 5 benchmark announcement](https://blog.voltaic.gg/announcing-the-voltaic-season-5-aiming-benchmarks-beta-for-kovaaks/).

## Common mistakes

- Assuming a high trainer benchmark rank means your in-game aim is equally good, when a trainer
  doesn't reproduce your game's recoil, hitboxes, or crosshair.
- Never practicing in a dedicated trainer because "only the game counts," which ignores that
  mechanics are the part of aim that transfers most directly and that a trainer gives you far more
  focused reps on them than a match does.
- Practicing crosshair placement and positioning only in a trainer, when both depend on the
  sightlines, angles, and abilities of the specific game you play.

## How to combine trainer and in-game practice

Use trainer time for mechanics and game time for everything a trainer can't reproduce.
[Voltaic](../resources/communities/voltaic.md) structures its own [routines](../training/routines.md)
around this same split: general Fundamental Routines for overall mouse control, separate from
game-specific routines built around the aspects of aiming a given game leans on most, per its
[Getting Started guide](https://blog.voltaic.gg/getting-started-with-voltaic/). Both
[KovaaK's](../resources/trainers/kovaaks.md) and [Aimlabs](../resources/trainers/aimlabs.md)
similarly offer game-specific benchmarks and training programs alongside their general scenario
libraries. A short trainer warm-up before you queue, plus a regular routine that targets a
specific weakness, still needs time in the actual game to turn into crosshair placement,
positioning, and game sense you can rely on in a match.
