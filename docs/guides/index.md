Guides are signed, first-person pages: how to attack a specific problem, how to break a hard
scenario into parts you can actually train, how to get unstuck on a scenario that has stalled.

They sit outside the [wiki](../wiki/index.md) on purpose, because they run on a different trust
model. A wiki page earns trust by citing a public source for each claim, and anyone can correct it.
A guide earns trust by carrying its author's name. That is the whole model: if you trust the person
who wrote it, the guide is worth your time, and if you do not, it is not. Judge it on the name, and
on whether the method works when you try it.

Guides are opinions, so expect them to disagree with each other eventually, and to disagree with
how you already train. Nothing here overrides the wiki's sourced pages on
[Fundamentals](../wiki/fundamentals/index.md) or [Skills](../wiki/skills/index.md); a guide is one
person's route through that material, not a correction to it.

## Writing one

Guides are written by this site's maintainer. Authorship is not open to contributors, so open an
issue on the [source repository](https://github.com/Bassel-Bakr/aim) rather than a pull request
that adds one. Corrections to a published guide, such as a factual error, a dead link, or a typo,
are welcome from anyone; changes to the method or the argument go to the author, because the byline
has to keep meaning what it says.

The wiki is the opposite: it is open to contributions, and
[CONTRIBUTING](https://github.com/Bassel-Bakr/aim/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) covers how to add or
change a page there.
